Ancient Greece.

Clothes
Hair was curled. Many of the clothes were made by the women and female slaves.
Wealthy people had tunics made from coloured cloth.
They wore light loose clothes made from linen in the summer and wool in the winter.
They were made from large squares of cloth held in place by pins.

Schools

Greek schools were small and had only ten to twenty pupils for each teacher.
Schooling had to be paid for and only the rich were educated.
They wrote on wooden boards covered with wax.
Boys were educated to be good citizens and to look after after affairs of state.
Girls education was for housekeeping and looking after the family.

Theatres

The theatres were built on hillsides in the open air and could often hold more than 18000 spectators.
Plays were either spoken or sung in rhyme. 
 
Sports

The Greeks believed that a healthy body was important.
Most men and boys practiced sports every day because they enjoyed them and wanted to keep fit.
The Greek armies had to be fit enough to march long distances carrying all their heavy equipment and to fight the enemy at the end of the journey.
The Greeks had four sports festivals where athletes from city states  competed against one another.
Most important was the Olympic Games. They were played in Olympia every four years.
